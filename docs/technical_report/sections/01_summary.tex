\section{Introduction}

Four-bit floating-point (FP4) Tensor Cores accelerate general matrix
multiplication (GEMM), but each training layer must also produce scales, pack
values, build forward and backward layouts, and retain autograd state. When
separate kernels move bfloat16 (BF16) intermediates through high-bandwidth
memory (HBM), this surrounding work can erase the GEMM speedup
\cite{nvidia2025nvfp4}.

We address this gap with \emph{format-aware fusion}: each producer completes
its format-specific scale domain while emitting the layouts and saved state
required by its consumers. \mxfp{} completes a local 32-value scale, global
\nvfp{} waits for a tensor-wide maximum, and cooperative-thread-array-local
(CTA-local) \nvfp{} completes two outer scales inside the tile that owns the
matrix multiplication. The profitable fusion boundary therefore differs by
format.

We evaluate the resulting paths in Llama-3-family 8B pretraining at sequence length
8,192 and global batch 512. Same-accelerator probes measure every route with a
BF16 output projection and compiled cross entropy (CE), while long
trajectories cover up to 160B tokens. The fastest custom route reaches 37.9K
tokens/s/GPU. The \mxfp{} route with row-gradient stochastic rounding (SR)
and fixed-sign H32 weight-gradient (Wgrad) preconditioning reaches 37.2K
tokens/s/GPU and ends 2.11\% above the raw BF16 endpoint. The TE-native and
four-final-BF16-block trajectories end 1.34\% and 0.87\% above raw BF16,
respectively, at measured throughputs of 27.6K and 27.1K tokens/s/GPU.

Relative to the established methods summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:prior-work-boundary}, we contribute:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Numerical parameterization and operand contracts.} We make the
        \mxfp{} E8M0 scale direction an explicit route parameter: reported
        activation and gradient operands use upward, encode-centric scaling,
        while 2D32 weight tiles conditionally select a twice-finer scale under
        a fixed tile-maximum test. We integrate established
        orientation-consistent $16\!\times\!16$ and $32\!\times\!32$ weight
        encodings (2D16 and 2D32) into the custom route contracts. Global v5
        returns both packed weight views under one 2D16 encoding; MXFP4 and
        CTA-local fused producers emit both consumer layouts from one numerical
        encoding; and the operand hybrid emits distinct encodings from one BF16
        weight-tile load. We also separate the two representations of $dY$:
        adjacent-bin SR acts only on the row-facing data gradient (Dgrad),
        while Wgrad uses deterministic column-facing carriers
        and, in designated routes, paired fixed-sign H16 or H32
        preconditioning. The rounding rules, 2D consistency principle, SR
        estimator, and Hadamard transform are established; our contribution is
        their operand-specific parameterization and fused composition.

  \item \emph{Format-aware fusion and native execution.} We implement native
        \mxfp{}, tensor-global \nvfp{}, and \localcta{} around their distinct
        scale-completion boundaries. The producers fuse or overlap
        RMSNorm--QKV--RoPE and gated-FFN forward and backward work while
        emitting scale metadata, retained state, and the physical layouts
        required by each consumer. Global v5 overlaps the tensor-wide maximum;
        \localcta{} instead carries two tile-owned FP32 outer factors across
        the contraction and applies their product in the GEMM epilogue.

  \item \emph{End-to-end empirical evaluation.} We implement and evaluate the
        custom pure and hybrid routes alongside BF16 and TE comparators. The
        hybrid designs include depth-wise format assignment and an operand-wise
        assignment motivated by same-state Dgrad error analysis. The evidence
        comprises matched throughput probes, trajectories through 160B
        training tokens, checkpoint-matched fixed-stream validation, and
        downstream evaluation of the complete recipes.

  \item \emph{Public reproducibility release.} We release the training and
        evaluation code, pinned kernel sources, complete recipe ledger,
        publication data, and figure builders. Appendix~\ref{app:code-release}
        describes the repository and its reproducibility contract.
\end{itemize}
