\section{Conclusion and Further Work}

Format-aware fusion makes the complete FP4 producer--consumer boundary the
unit of optimization. The TE routes give the closest observed training-loss
tracking, with measured tensor-global execution paths at 27.1--27.6K
tokens/s/GPU. Among the custom routes, \mxfp{} with row-gradient SR and
fixed-sign H32 Wgrad preconditioning reaches 37.2K tokens/s/GPU, 86.3\%
BF16 MFU, a 2.11\% raw
endpoint-loss excess, and the lowest observed validation NLL on the fixed
external data stream. Its
downstream ranking remains task dependent. BF16 records the highest MMLU
score, the fixed-H32 operand hybrid the highest WinoGrande score, and
\mxfp{} with row-SR and fixed H32 the highest HellaSwag and ARC-Challenge
scores. In this panel, late training loss and downstream accuracy therefore do
not have a monotonic ordering; training loss alone does not rank the downstream
quality of these complete low-precision recipes.

Neither the tested depth-wise nor operand-wise hybrid improves the combined
speed--quality point of the strongest pure custom route. The opposite outcomes
of Hadamard preconditioning under \mxfp{} and CTA-local scaling motivate
choosing the group of values sharing each scale, the rounding rule, and the
preconditioner jointly. A complementary direction is to change the scale
representation itself. UE5M3 FP4 pairs E2M1 payloads with an unsigned E5M3
block scale and has supported a recipe with periodic tensor-scale updates,
selective SR, and no Hadamard preconditioning on a separate model and training
setup \cite{hu2026ue5m3}. Combining wider unsigned block scales with a
CTA-local producer schedule, or with delayed tensor scaling and global outer
scales, is a natural next step.
