\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Step speed is not time-to-quality}

Let $R_0$ be baseline throughput and $N_0$ the tokens required to reach a
predeclared quality target. If FP4 improves throughput by $g$ but requires
$\tau$ more tokens, then
\begin{equation}
  \frac{T_{\mathrm{FP4}}}{T_0}=\frac{1+\tau}{1+g}.
  \label{eq:time-to-quality}
\end{equation}
FP4 wins only when the throughput gain exceeds the additional tokens required.
The measured routes differ in both throughput and loss trajectory. Computing
their time-to-quality ratio still requires a predeclared quality target; a
training-loss gap at one step is not enough.

\subsection{System and numerical choices interact}

The measured TE routes track BF16 closely: TE-native and the
four-final-BF16-block route end 1.34\% and 0.87\% above the raw BF16 loss.
Their common probes reach 27.6K and 27.1K tokens/s/GPU. A separate
same-accelerator probe of the custom global \nvfp{} route reaches 32.5K
tokens/s/GPU; its long trajectory ends 2.69\% above BF16. TE-native \nvfp{}
and custom global \nvfp{} both use the tensor-global NVFP4 scale hierarchy;
the four-final-BF16-block route uses that hierarchy in its first 28 blocks.
The panel does not isolate the cost
of scaling from implementation and batch geometry, but it exposes the systems
constraint: a tensor-global maximum-absolute-value reduction creates a
completion dependency that the execution path must hide or pay.

Hadamard preconditioning is not uniformly beneficial across scale strategies.
The fixed-H32 \mxfp{} recipe retains 98.34\% of row-SR-only \mxfp{}
throughput while moving the raw endpoint excess from 6.53\% to 2.11\% and
validation NLL from 2.593146 to 2.422804. The fixed-H16 CTA-local recipe is
slower than CTA-local without the transform and has higher endpoint and
validation loss. These are comparisons between complete trajectories, not
experiments that isolate the effect of Hadamard preconditioning alone.

Neither tested hybrid dominates the strongest pure custom route. The
depth-wise hybrid and both operand-wise hybrids are slower and have higher
endpoint and validation loss than \mxfp{} with row-SR and fixed H32. A
controlled replay on identical saved tensors still finds lower Dgrad error for
CTA-local in all 288 tested layer--projection comparisons. The completed hybrid
trajectories do not preserve that operator-level advantage in endpoint or
validation loss.

\subsection{Wider block scales are a complementary direction}

A separate direction is to change the scale representation rather than mix
MXFP4 and CTA-local NVFP4. UE5M3 FP4 pairs E2M1 payloads with an unsigned
E5M3 block scale. Hu et al. report end-to-end Nemotron-H 8B pretraining with
per-operand tensor scales that are updated every 50 steps and held fixed
between updates, selective SR on backward gradients, no RHT, and FP4 in all
eligible internal linears
\cite{hu2026ue5m3}. This is a software-emulated block-scale path on a different
model and training setup, not one of the native Llama routes measured here.
It nevertheless shows that a wider set of representable scale values can
support a recipe with periodic scaling and without RHT. Native UE5M3 combined
with a CTA-local producer schedule, or with delayed tensor scaling and global
outer scales, is an untested next step.

\subsection{Limitations}

Each long-run recipe is a single trajectory. Some resumes change local-batch
and gradient-accumulation settings while preserving the global batch, and the
fixed-sign Hadamard routes differ from their controls in more than
preconditioning alone. These comparisons therefore describe complete recipes
rather than isolating the effect of one design choice. The controlled
identical-tensor diagnostic covers one checkpoint and three sequence-2,048
batches.
It replays the linear operators without attention mixing, residual paths, or
RoPE, so it does not establish a universal layer-sensitivity rule.
Training-loss observations for custom global NVFP4 are sparse after step
19,000, and its dashed late curve is a display-only fit evaluated at the BF16
plotting steps. The fixed validation
stream is not a proven zero-overlap split from every historical training
trajectory. These limits govern the claims throughout the report.
