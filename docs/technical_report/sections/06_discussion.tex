\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Step speed is not time-to-quality}

Let $R_0$ be baseline throughput and $N_0$ the tokens required to reach a
predeclared quality target. If FP4 improves throughput by $g$ but requires
$\tau$ more tokens, then
\begin{equation}
  \frac{T_{\mathrm{FP4}}}{T_0}=\frac{1+\tau}{1+g}.
  \label{eq:time-to-quality}
\end{equation}
FP4 wins only when the throughput gain exceeds the additional tokens required.
The measured routes differ in both throughput and loss trajectory. Computing
their time-to-quality ratio still requires a predeclared quality target; a
training-loss gap at one step is not enough.

\subsection{System and numerical choices interact}

The measured TE routes track BF16 closely: TE-native and the
four-final-BF16-block route end 1.34\% and 0.87\% above the raw BF16 loss.
Their common probes reach 27.6K and 27.1K tokens/s/GPU. The fused global-v5
route retains the tensor-global \nvfp{} scale hierarchy and reaches 32.5K
tokens/s/GPU, but ends 2.69\% above BF16. The panel does not isolate the cost
of scaling from implementation and batch geometry, but it exposes the systems
constraint: a tensor-global amax creates a completion dependency that the
execution path must hide or pay.

Hadamard preconditioning is not uniformly beneficial across scale strategies.
The fixed-H32 \mxfp{} recipe retains 98.34\% of row-SR-only \mxfp{}
throughput while moving the raw endpoint excess from 6.53\% to 2.11\% and
validation NLL from 2.593146 to 2.422804. The fixed-H16 CTA-local recipe is
slower than CTA-local without the transform and has higher endpoint and
validation loss. These are comparisons between complete trajectories, not
one-factor ablations.

Neither tested hybrid dominates the strongest pure custom route. The
depth-wise hybrid and both operand-wise hybrids are slower and have higher
endpoint and validation loss than \mxfp{}-H32. The same-state replay still
finds lower Dgrad error for CTA-local in every measured cell. Local operator
error can therefore motivate a candidate, but the full training trajectory
adjudicates the recipe.

\subsection{Wider block scales are a complementary direction}

A separate direction is to change the scale representation rather than mix
the two formats evaluated here. UE5M3 FP4 pairs E2M1 payloads with an unsigned
E5M3 block scale. Hu et al. report end-to-end Nemotron-H 8B pretraining with
per-operand, 50-step periodic sample-and-hold tensor scaling, selective SR on
backward gradients, no RHT, and FP4 in all eligible internal linears
\cite{hu2026ue5m3}. This is a software-emulated block-scale path on a different
model and training setup, not one of the native Llama routes measured here.
It nevertheless shows that a wider block-scale codebook can support a recipe
with periodic scaling and without RHT. Native UE5M3 combined with a CTA-local
producer schedule, or with delayed tensor scaling and global outer scales, is
an untested next step.

\subsection{Limitations}

Each long-run recipe is a single trajectory. Some resumes change LB/GA while
preserving the global batch, and the fixed-sign Hadamard routes differ from
their controls in more than preconditioning alone. These comparisons therefore
describe complete recipes rather than one-factor causal effects. The
same-state diagnostic covers one checkpoint and three sequence-2,048 batches.
It replays the linear operators without attention mixing, residual paths, or
RoPE, so it does not establish a universal layer-sensitivity rule.
Pure-v5 telemetry is sparse after step 19,000, and its dashed late curve is only
a visual fit through exact observations. The fixed validation stream is not a
proven zero-overlap split from every historical training trajectory. These
limits govern the claims throughout the report. The historical BF16 and
TE-native seeds were not recovered. Their plotted curves use early-run
seed-alignment offsets; Table~\ref{tab:training-endpoints} reports the
unadjusted observations.
